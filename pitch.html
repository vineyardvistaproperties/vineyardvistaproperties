Tourism Market

A deeply established seasonal market where the right property, disciplined pricing and exceptional management matter more than destination-level growth alone.

Martha’s Vineyard has a longstanding tradition of multigenerational summer visits and a well-established market for private home rentals. The opportunity for Vineyard Vista is to own a distinctive residence in a desirable location, generate seasonal rental income to offset annual carrying costs, and retain the home’s long-term personal and investment value.

Summer demand and seasonality

June through August anchors the home-rental season, with holiday weeks and desirable summer dates commanding particular attention. Pricing and booking behavior can differ substantially between early summer, peak weeks and late August. Rental projections should be built week by week rather than using one assumed rate across the season.

Positioning within the rental market

Location in Edgartown or Katama, proximity to beaches and village amenities, a pool, bedroom and bathroom capacity, and well-designed outdoor space can influence the audience and achievable rate. A larger house is not necessarily a better rental investment if its amenities or condition are less competitive.

Comparable rentals and realistic rates

Island rental agencies, including Tea Lane Associates with a portfolio of more than 300 Vineyard rental homes, market everything from modest cottages to significant family compounds at very different weekly prices. Each potential acquisition should be tested against current, closely matched rental listings and local agent guidance, adjusted for amenities, minimum-stay requirements, calendar availability, fees and expected booked weeks.

How rentals support ownership

The financial model compares 12 rented summer weeks with nine rented weeks and one personal summer week per partner. In either case, rental receipts offset mortgage and operating expenses; the partners still fund a predictable annual ownership cost. No initial profit distributions are assumed.

The underwriting case rests on the rental appeal of the specific home—not an assumption that island-wide visitor growth guarantees bookings or profitability.

How annual costs are calculated

(Annual mortgage principal and interest + annual operating expenses − realized summer rental income) ÷ 3 partners. The presentation models 7.25% interest and 30-year amortization. Individual loan balances, expenses and rental assumptions differ by acquisition scenario.

Important underwriting constraints

The model assumes a total 5% purchase-price cash commitment, allocated equally among the three partners. If not required as a down payment under actual loan terms, the presentation proposes retaining it as LLC reserve capital. The financing structure is illustrative; qualification and reserve requirements must be confirmed with a lender.

Lower realized rent, unbooked weeks, higher insurance or taxes, major repairs, and owner use each increase contributions. The model does not include appreciation or tax deductions as current cash flow.

Martha’s Vineyard considerations

Property acquisition and operation on the Island involve distinctive closing, diligence, insurance and ongoing maintenance requirements.

2% Land Bank transfer fee

A buyer-paid transfer fee materially increases cash required at closing and should be incorporated into acquisition planning.

Septic / Title 5 requirements

Many properties rely on septic systems; system condition, compliance and capacity should be part of diligence.

Water & well diligence

Depending on the property, water source, well quality and related infrastructure require specific review.

Coastal & property insurance

Wind, flood and coastal exposure can affect availability, deductibles and annual insurance cost.

Higher maintenance & logistics costs

Island labor, materials, deliveries and contractor availability can make repairs and routine upkeep more expensive or slower.

Seasonal home considerations

Winterization, storm preparation, off-season monitoring and year-round property management should be planned from day one.

Town-specific rules matter

Permitting, rental rules and property requirements vary by town and should be confirmed for the specific acquisition.

Financial objective: predictable, affordable annual partner contributions, supported by summer rent and long-term real-estate value—not a day-one distribution stream.

Rental Scenarios

Two modeled operating scenarios; no guaranteed three-week summer allocation to each owner.

Owner use is a deliberate economic trade-off. Each reserved personal summer week removes a potentially rentable week from the calendar and raises the partners’ annual net ownership cost.

12-week rental scenario

Rent all 12 modeled summer weeks. No personal summer weeks are included in this scenario. The modeled rental receipts reduce, but do not eliminate, annual partner contributions.

9-week owner-use scenario

Rent nine summer weeks and allocate one week of personal summer use to each of the three partners. Personal weeks are used without an additional rental charge, but the foregone rent is reflected in the higher modeled annual contribution.

Off-season and holidays

Calendar priority and off-season owner use are subject to partner-approved scheduling, operating rules and property availability. No summer holiday week is promised outside the selected rental scenario.

Annual calendar approval

Partners determine the summer rental and owner-use allocation before the booking season and underwrite expected costs accordingly. Actual rental results may change the annual funding requirement.

Modeled choice, not guaranteed income: The 9-week scenario carries a higher annual owner cost because of three reserved personal weeks. Both scenarios remain negative-current-carry cases, with no partner distributions.
The choice between nine and twelve rental weeks is an explicit trade-off between personal summer use and the annual cost of ownership.

Operations and Governance

Clear operating standards and decision rules protect the residence, the partnership and long-term ownership.

Property operations

Peak-season maintenance

Weekly professional turnover cleaning throughout June, July and August is a shared operating expense whether the week is used by an owner or rented.

Off-season responsibility

Off-season owner use requires 24 hours’ notice when available. Cleaning and guest-related expenses belong to the owner using or facilitating the stay.

All rentals through the LLC

No cash rentals, side agreements, private listings or informal paid stays. Every rental uses the LLC booking system, lease, insurance, deposits and payment processor.

Deposits, damages and rules

Renter damage is applied first against security deposits. The Pet Addendum and Vineyard Vista Properties Gathering Addendum to Rental Agreement apply where relevant. Indoor smoking, vaping and marijuana are prohibited.

Professional management

The property manager handles turnovers, vendors, access codes, rental logistics and guest coordination. Necessary repairs up to $1,500 may be authorized.

Consistency among friends

Uniform rules protect the property, preserve insurance coverage, maintain clean accounting and avoid awkward exceptions between owners.

One operating standard

All rentals, regardless of who is entitled to the week, must flow through the approved LLC booking and insurance framework.

Partner governance

Decision making

Up to $1,500: property manager or managing member. $1,501–$10,000: majority vote. Over $10,000: unanimous approval.

Transfers and exits

Co-owners receive a right of first refusal. One owner may buy the 1/3 interest, or the remaining owners may split it and convert to 50/50 ownership.

Inheritance and trusts

Transfers to revocable trusts are permitted for estate planning. Heirs remain subject to the LLC agreement, obligations, transfer rules and right of first refusal.

Annual meeting

Owners review financials, reserve balance, rental pricing, calendar, property manager performance and capital needs each year.
